Project 1 · Decisions

Six choices that change the answer.

01

Drop transfers and AMA

Their outcome happens elsewhere. Counting them as survivors rewards hospitals that transfer sick patients.

02

Only what care can’t change

Stay length and procedures stay out. Severity codes are a grey zone, so an admission-only model runs as the other extreme.

03

Folds split by hospital

Out-of-fold predictions: no hospital is scored by a model that trained on its own patients.

04

Calibration over accuracy

About 1 in 8 die. No resampling: expected deaths are a sum of probabilities, so they must be honest.

05

Funnel plot, not rankings

Small hospitals get wider limits. A league table overreacts to noise in small numbers.

06

Race out of the model

Used only to check calibration by group, so the adjustment can’t excuse unequal outcomes.

05

Likely interview questions sit here: missing data (redacted hospital IDs dropped, outcome-unknown discharges dropped), imbalance (12.6% base rate, no resampling), leakage (grouped folds, no care-process features). Model inputs: age group, sex, admission type, ED admission, APR severity of illness, APR risk of mortality. The APR scores are assigned at discharge from all coded diagnoses, so they can absorb complications and coding habits. Kept because admission factors alone give AUROC 0.60. Also dropped two kinds of hospital: mostly-elective (palliative, rehab) and one reporting zero deaths among 100+ cases.

Project 1 · Limits and next steps

A signal worth a look, not a verdict.

Bayesian shrinkage

A random intercept per hospital pulls small hospitals toward the average; compare with the funnel flags.

Stability over time

Repeat for 2022: one bad year and a persistent problem look the same.

Beyond in-hospital death

30-day mortality needs linked death records; hospice discharge is the stand-in for now.

Coding, not care

Mild sepsis ranges from 4% to 38% of a hospital’s cases. Present-on-admission flags would separate coding from care.

Project 3 · Decisions

An agent you can debug.

Deterministic final say

The rule-based scorer ranks every result, so the same request gives the same list and tests can pin it down.

Three retrieval modes

Structured filters, semantic search or a hybrid of both; the agent picks one per request.

A check, not blind trust

Confidence plus flags. Ask for a fake genre and it returns nothing at confidence 0.00, rather than a guess.

Built to fail safely

Capped retry loop, every LLM call logged, a templated fallback, and 24 offline tests with mocked LLM calls.
